Hypotheses · pending validation

What we expect to find

Owner-formed hypotheses based on direct experience as an SE at Hivelocity. Each will be validated, refined, or rejected through peer interviews before being treated as findings.

  • H-01
    SEs spend more time data-hunting than solutioning
    The same data fragmentation pattern observed in CSM (UberSmith / HubSpot / ContractWorks / Confluence / Slack across 5–7 systems) likely affects SE workflow even more acutely, since SEs need technical data alongside customer data.
  • H-02
    Proposals are mostly manual reconstruction from precedent
    Without a live price sheet (Decision D-04 from CSM track) and without a structured SKU library, every proposal is partially rebuilt from prior deals. Estimated 60–80% of proposal work is re-formatting prior content, not net-new technical thinking.
  • H-03
    Configuration sanity checking is informal and risk-prone
    No automated check that proposed configurations are physically deployable, contractually compliant, or pricing-coherent. SEs catch errors via experience, not systems. Errors that escape detection become customer-facing incidents post-sale.
  • H-04
    Knowledge access during calls is "ask Kelly or Slack"
    During live customer technical conversations, SEs cannot reliably look up DC capacity, networking config, RAID compatibility, etc. without putting the customer on hold or following up later. Same Confluence-is-stale problem CSM has, with higher cost.
  • H-05
    SE-to-PM handoff loses critical customer context
    Once a deal closes, SE context (objections, technical preferences, decision criteria, customer's actual reasoning) does not consistently transfer to PMO for deployment. Causes deployment friction and CSM onboarding gaps downstream.

DEPT-12 · STRATEGIC VISION · HIP MASTER PLAN

Strategic Vision — Hivelocity Intelligence Platform

The umbrella that governs every department track. The Hivelocity Intelligence Platform (HIP) is a shared AI and data backbone underneath every department's tool. Each department gets a tool tailored to its workflow; HIP gives those tools shared customer identity, model services, governance, and the cross-team workflows that make the federation cohere.

● UMBRELLA · ACTIVE CROSS-CUTTING 3 PHASES · 90 DAYS PHASE 1 BUDGET · $0

The thesis

Hivelocity does not need to buy a customer success platform, a CRM enrichment tool, an SDR automation suite, an AM intelligence product, and an ops console. It needs one architecture with department-specific tool surfaces and a shared backbone — built incrementally, proven on one department, then federated across the company.

The reasoning is not novelty; it is reality. A six-month integration of Gainsight or Totango against the current data fragmentation will fail at the data layer before the tool can deliver value. The data has to get clean before any platform works. That cleanup is itself the product. Build it first.

This master plan is intentionally vendor-neutral. Any qualified integration partner — internal hire, external agency, or hybrid — can pick this up and execute it without re-doing foundational discovery.

Three phases, ninety days

The same three phases proposed in the CSM track scale to the company. Each phase has a clear exit criterion before the next begins.

PHASE 01
Stabilize
Days 0 – 30
Pilot Customer Pulse with Kelly. Manual paste-in flows, AI-driven structuring, summarizing, drafting. Built in Notion + Claude API. No IT dependencies, no integration work, no budget request. Exit criterion: Kelly reclaims 60%+ of QBR prep time. Other CSM tracks see the demo and want their version next.
PHASE 02
Integrate
Days 30 – 90
Build HIP read-layer. Read-only API integrations into HubSpot, Zendesk, UberSmith. Customer Pulse becomes live data. Roll out to Bob, Diana, Abel. Trustworthy customer health score deployed. Exit criterion: CSM team unanimously trusts and uses health score; signal feed catches at least one would-be churn before cancel.
PHASE 03
Scale
Days 90+
Federate across departments. Each track gets its own tool surface on the HIP backbone. SE, SDR, AM, SalesOps next. Operations tracks (Support, Billing, Product, Marketing, etc.) follow with their own discovery cycles. Exit criterion: Build vs. buy decision finally made with clean data.

Architectural principles

Six principles that govern every implementation decision across the engagement.

01
Federated tools, shared backbone
Each department owns its tool. The backbone (HIP) is shared. This avoids the trap of forcing one tool to fit every department's workflow.
02
Read before write
Phase 2 integrations are read-only. HIP consumes from systems of record without writing back until trust and governance are established. Reduces blast radius of early failures.
03
Manual paste before API
Phase 1 deliberately uses paste-in workflows. Forces clarity on what data actually matters before integration cost is incurred. Many integrations get scoped that turn out to be unnecessary once the workflow is real.
04
Kelly first, then pattern
One pilot user, one department, one tool. Prove the pattern. Then federate. Avoids the org-wide rollout failure mode of every CRM and CS platform implementation in history.
05
Vendor-neutral by construction
All deliverables are written so any qualified integration partner can pick them up without re-doing discovery. No partner-specific architectural assumptions.
06
Honest scoping
Five departments at deep scope (where credibility exists). Six at strategic-framing scaffold (where it does not). One umbrella. Acknowledging the boundary is the most credible thing the plan can do.

What HIP is, concretely

HIP is not a single product. It is the named architectural layer that contains everything the department tools depend on but should not each implement separately.

Core HIP capabilities

·
Unified customer identity
One customer record per customer, reconciled across UberSmith, HubSpot, Zendesk, ContractWorks, MyVelocity portal, and Slack mentions. Every department tool reads this identity, never reconstructs it.
·
Model layer · AI as a service
Shared call-summarization, recap-drafting, signal-detection, and report-generation models. Department tools call shared services rather than each tool integrating its own AI vendor.
·
Knowledge layer
Kelly's cheat sheet, rebuilt as a governed AI-queryable knowledge base. Every department tool can ask the same knowledge layer the same questions and get the same answers. Owned, refreshed, versioned.
·
Signal feed
Cross-system event stream — going-quiet, ticket spikes, sentiment shifts, billing flags, intent signals. Each department subscribes to the signals relevant to its tool's purpose.
·
Governance & audit
Single access control plane, single audit log, single data-handling policy. Required for any AI deployment touching customer data; cheaper to centralize than to per-tool replicate.
·
Cross-team workflows
When a customer signal in one department's tool implies action in another (CSM detects expansion intent → SDR or AM acts), HIP routes it. The federation works because HIP carries the connective tissue.

The handoff question

This plan is designed for a partner to execute. Three viable ownership models exist. The right answer depends on Hivelocity-internal factors outside this engagement's scope; the recommendation below is the operator's view.

Option A — Internal hire

Hivelocity hires a dedicated AI/automation lead and they execute Phase 1 and 2. Pro: institutional knowledge stays in-house. Con: 6–9 month time-to-productivity, heavy recruiting risk for a niche skillset.

Option B — External integration partner

A specialist agency executes Phase 1 and 2 against this plan. Pro: fastest time-to-value, partner owns delivery risk. Con: ongoing dependency unless internal capability is built in parallel.

Option C — Hybrid

External partner builds Phase 1 and 2 with an internal owner shadowing for ongoing operation. Pro: speed of partner + institutional knowledge transfer + reduced ongoing dependency. Con: requires a specific internal hire and a partner willing to enable rather than retain.

Operator's recommendation: Option C, with Phase 1 led by an integration partner and an internal SalesOps or Revenue-Tech hire shadowing from week one. Phase 2 transitions ownership progressively. Phase 3 is internally owned with partner on retainer for new department onboardings.
